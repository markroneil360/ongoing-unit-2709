Why this cannot be dismissed as a single spike

The qualifying record contains repeated events lasting from 15 minutes to 161 minutes. The recurrence and duration justify independent environmental and occupational-health investigation.

Analogy: this is like a smoke detector repeatedly activating for long intervals. The record is enough to require investigation, while the detector alone cannot identify the source or measure a person's dose.
What “dominant” means

A spectral vote, not a single pure tone

For each complete minute, mean spectral power density in 4–8 Hz was compared with 1–4, 8–16 and 16–20 Hz. A minute entered this dataset only when 4–8 Hz won that comparison.

Analogy: imagine four shelves holding parts of the same signal. “4–8 Hz dominant” means that shelf held the largest share—not that every item was on it.

WHO nighttime health context

Why prolonged nighttime noise matters

WHO environmental-noise guidance recommends less than 30 dB(A) in bedrooms at night for good-quality sleep and identifies environmental noise as a contributor to sleep disturbance and longer-term health burdens including cardiovascular effects. The R6E8A dashboard therefore gives sustained nighttime and early-morning events priority rather than allowing short normal periods to dominate the presentation.

  • Sleep: repeated or sustained nighttime noise can disturb sleep.
  • Physiological burden: WHO identifies cardiovascular effects among recognized environmental-noise health concerns.
  • Duration matters: management review should distinguish multi-hour activity from isolated peaks.

WHO's bedroom figure is A-weighted and is not numerically interchangeable with an unweighted 4–8 Hz or 16 Hz HDF band value. The health context is therefore shown without a fabricated percentage.
