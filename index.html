Night-noise human-health context

The World Health Organization states that its expert group reviewed scientific evidence on the health effects of night noise and developed health-based guideline values intended to protect the public. For legal review, this supports treating repeated nighttime environmental noise as a health-relevant exposure context, not merely an annoyance.

Source: WHO Regional Office for Europe, Night Noise Guidelines for Europe (2009).
Low-frequency vibration exposure context

A NASA technical review of ISO 2631 describes adverse whole-body vibration effects ranging from back troubles and other pathological problems to impaired performance and discomfort. This is related human-exposure context for why sustained low-frequency activity warrants professional assessment; it does not by itself establish that the HDF pressure signal caused a specific medical effect.

Source: NASA technical report, Ride Quality and International Standard ISO 2631.

WHO · Night-noise and sleep-health context

WHO nighttime guidance remains the health-context benchmark. Its A-weighted night-noise values are not numerically interchangeable with unweighted 4–8 Hz HDF levels; the dashboard therefore uses WHO to explain nighttime health relevance while keeping the ordinance and acoustic measurements methodologically separate.

ISO 7196 · Infrasound screening context

ISO 7196 G-weighted assessment remains the dedicated infrasound-screening framework for approximately 1–20 Hz pressure data. Unweighted HDF band values are not relabeled as dB(G). This keeps the infrasound benchmark visible without overstating comparability.

STC / IBC · Building-envelope context

STC and relevant IBC hotel/building provisions remain building-design and transmission context, not direct 4–8 Hz sensor thresholds. They help a reviewer understand the expected role of partitions, assemblies and building systems while avoiding a false one-to-one conversion from HDF pressure data.
